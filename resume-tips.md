# From the Desk of Dr. Vanessa Z. Chan, Penn Engineering Vice Dean of Innovation and Entrepreneurship

Recruiters & employers spend on average 30-60 seconds on a résumé. Given this, it is important that your résumé is easy to read, tells a story and is interesting enough that they want to meet you to learn more. I consistently see the same set of issues in student résumés, so here’s a tip sheet on ways to make your résumé more impactful.

## Make it Easy to Read

There’s a popular format that recruiters/employers are familiar with, which means they will be able to get more out of reading your résumé in 30-60 seconds if you put it into this familiar format.

## Focus on “Impact”

Many of the student résumés that I’ve read focus on a list of activities - I did X, I did Y, but these list of activities do not communicate the “impact” you had or “why what you did matters”. As such, these list of activities do not tell a story. As an example of what I often see:

- Worked on Python model and optimized code to improve output
- Led analysis and simulations using applets and crunched data for clinical files

The set of bullets above are very flat, and it’s unclear what the impact was that you had and why you worked on these activities. It would be better if you could write something like:

- Owned optimization of Python model for tumor-targeting nanoparticles, laying groundwork to develop personalized cancer treatments
- Led analysis and redesign of simulation - achieved 66% increase in computing speed for biophysical targeted-nanoparticle model
- Organized collaboration between clinical and engineering research groups which led to understanding of liver cell biomechanics in context of drug delivery
- Developed user-friendly applet in R, solving data-accessibility problem by analyzing 32k clinical files for 60+ types of cancer, allowing researchers to efficiently visualize deadliest gene variants and streamline targeted nanoparticle development

## Avoid Anything Generic

If your bullet point can apply to harvesting coffee in Costa Rica, it’s too generic. For example:

- Created a technology improvement roadmap; proposed recommendations accepted by the EC and board of directors
- Assessed IT and financial operating performance through management interviews and financial benchmarking
- Implemented recommendations including organizational redesign and new business processes

It would be better if you can be specific on the impact and share details in the story to bring it to life. For example:

- Worked directly with the Chief Digital Officer and CIO to assess issues with current composite technology processes and developed a new technology improvement roadmap that cut commercialization time by 30% which could improve ROI by 50%.
- New roadmap required significant changes in current workflow, so interviewed senior managers to build buy-in and created financial benchmarks (ROI, NPV) and operating metrics (up-time, response time) to convince EC and BoD to accept transformational recommendations
- Rolled out roadmap including an organizational design (delayering by 2) and IT business processes.

## Extracurricular or Volunteer Experiences

In this section, it’s important to showcase how you’ve played a leadership role and be specific on impact and things that you’ve done. How have you built something? How have you had an impact on the organization or individuals that you have worked on? Also be specific and not generic. Include the amounts of the dollars that you may have raised, how many people are in your organization, how often you have practice or meet? How has your team done if you are playing a sport? Even if you haven’t won, have you guys improved and how did you help drive that? If you launched something for high school students, what impact did that have on them? Remember, you are telling a story so you need these details so the reader can paint a picture. If you are a first year, think about what you want your story to be when you graduate and start building it! An interesting candidate is not someone who has a 4.0 GPA and has taken a zillion classes but someone who has actually taken some risks, has pushed themselves to develop real world skills and have tried to have an impact in everything they do.

## Deword

Get rid of filler words and streamline by using active verbs. As an example, take something like this:

- Owned optimization of Python model for tumor-targeting nanoparticles, laying groundwork for next steps towards developing personalized cancer treatments.
- Led analysis and redesign of simulation, achieving 66% increase in computing speed for biophysical targeted-nanoparticle model.
- Organized collaboration between clinical and engineering research groups, leading to understanding of liver cell biomechanics in context of drug delivery.
- Developed user-friendly applet in R, solving data-accessibility problem by analyzing 32k clinical files for 60+ types of cancer, allowing researchers to efficiently visualize deadliest gene variants and streamline targeted nanoparticle development.

Get rid of superfluous words like, “laying, towards , leading to” and streamline to this:

- Optimized Python model for tumor-targeting nanoparticles, laying groundwork for personalized cancer treatments.
- Redesigned simulation, increasing computing speed by 66% for biophysical targeted-nanoparticle model.
- Organized collaboration between clinical and engineering research groups, to understand liver cell biomechanics in drug delivery.
- Developed user-friendly applet in R, solved data-accessibility problem by analyzing 32k clinical files for 60+ types of cancer, allowing researchers to efficiently visualize deadliest gene variants to streamline targeted nanoparticle development.

## Check for Grammar, Spelling, and Formatting

The devil is in the details so print out a clean sheet of your résumé and with a red pen, go through it with a fine tooth comb. Look for spelling or grammar issues, places where the bullets don’t line up, make sure each section is in reverse chronological order etc.. All the dates should be Right Justified and not out floating. Avoid acronyms that the person reading would not understand - spell them out. Make sure there are no silly mistakes in your résumé—employers don’t want to hire someone who is sloppy.

## Other

People ultimately also want to know if you are interesting so include any interesting facts about yourself at the end - do you mountain bike? Play a musical instrument? Make insane cakes? It’s about painting a full picture of you. Also include if you have lived in any foreign countries or speak foreign languages. Also include which country you are a citizen of and if you are looking for US based jobs, include what your visa or green card status

## Create a LinkedIn Profile

Recruiters and others often use LinkedIn to connect. Please create a LinkedIn profile and include it on your résumé in the line that with your contact information. It’s important to have a professional headshot.

## Scrub your Social Media

In an ideal situation, do not use your real name on social media platforms and use an alias. Recruiters and others now look on-line to see what else you are up to and I encourage all my students to use an alias on sites like Facebook, Instagram etc. You never know what someone might post and tag you to.

I hope these tips are helpful and remember, it’s ALL about the network. The most effective way to get a job is from connections, so leave the lab and classroom and dorm and take part in events outside in the real world. No matter how good your résumé is, it’s all about personal connections and referrals.

Happy job hunting!
